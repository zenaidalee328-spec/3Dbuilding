# Windmill: facts to verify before posting

- Saddle querns: earliest farming (~9000 BCE Abu Hureyra, Syria; Britain ~4000 BC). VERIFY (dates vary). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quern-stone · https://new.millsarchive.org/from-quern-to-computer-the-history-of-flour-milling/4
- Rotary hand quern: Britain ~400 BC; Carthage 6th c. BC. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_mill
- Donkey/horse mill: Carthaginian, 4th c. BC; Mallorca shipwreck 375-350 BC; Pompeii bakeries. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_mill · https://pompeiisites.org/en/?p=50244
- Earliest certain windmill in N. Europe: 1185 Weedley, Yorkshire; post mill = earliest European type. VERIFY (Weedley type debated). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill
- Post mill turned by a tail pole. Common sails = cloth on a lattice, reefed to the wind. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill_sail
- Brake friction set mills on fire (Great Storm 1703; Jill Windmill 1987). https://www.jillwindmill.org.uk/1987.htm
- Tower mill by 1295: only the cap turns. Smock mill: Dutch, 17th c. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_mill
- Tailwinding can lift a cap off (St Olaves). https://windmillworld.com/news/item581.htm
- Fantail: Edmund Lee, blacksmith, Brock Mill Forge near Wigan, patent 1745. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill_fantail · https://www.britannica.com/biography/Edmund-Lee

Staging, not history: "gorilla arms", "donkey eats half the wheat", "miller vanishes in flour" are jokes; sails on a fixed post is an invented wrong step; the order is simplified (windmills came ~1,500 years after the donkey mill). The final mill is NOT called Dutch (fantails were mainly English).
