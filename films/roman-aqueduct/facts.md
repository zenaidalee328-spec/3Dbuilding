# Roman Aqueduct: facts to check before posting

- Pont du Gard aqueduct (Nîmes): about 50 km long, falls about 12.6 m over its length: roughly 25 cm per km (the film says "twenty-five centimeters every kilometer").
- Aqueducts used gravity only, no pumps.
- Channels were covered (stone slabs / vaults) to keep water clean and cool and out of the sun.
- Valleys were crossed on arcades; the Pont du Gard has three tiers of arches.
- Vitruvius (De Architectura, Book 8): the water tank (castellum) splits into three: public basins/fountains, baths, private houses; in shortage the private supply fails first so the public never lacks.
- "Fill the valley with dirt / the rain washes it away": a joke step (not history).
- Valley depth, arch sizes and the slope in the film are drawn larger than life for clarity.
